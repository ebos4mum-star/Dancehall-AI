## 3. Can official YouTube embedding work in our Expo iOS app?

**Mostly yes technically; the constraints change the design.**

What I verified from snippets of YouTube's developer policies (full text unreachable, so confirm): the embedded player must reflect the standard YouTube experience; overriding the platform's rendering, hiding or blocking standard player features, and **covering the player or its controls with overlays** are restricted; the player must allow background play where the platform allows it; you must not restrict a viewer's ability to watch; requiring anything beyond pressing play is likely a violation. **Not found:** explicit clauses on caching or separating audio, but extracting streams is plainly outside the official player.

**Technology:** the official IFrame Player API in a web view. A community wrapper (`react-native-youtube-iframe`) exposes play, pause, seek, current time and state events and is reported to work with Expo. **Unverified:** compatibility with Expo SDK 58, the exact seek and time methods, and iOS web-view embedding requirements (YouTube has recently required a valid origin or referrer for embeds in web views, which breaks some setups with error 153; test on a device). It would add a native web-view dependency, so it needs a new native build.

**Consequences for the product**

1. **The video is a black box the app can steer but not alter.** We can play, pause and seek it. We cannot draw the timer ring **over** it (the current session screen puts the ring on the hero stage); the ring and controls must sit **outside** the player area.
2. **Ads and interruptions:** an embedded video may show ads or end-cards that we cannot remove, and the uploader controls them. A pre-roll would delay the class start.
3. **Availability:** the owner can delete the video, make it private or switch embedding off, and the class silently disappears. The guided-text fallback already handles this, but the class is not ours.
4. **Captions and accessibility** depend on the uploader's captions (we can ask the player to show captions by default).
5. **Privacy (Apple 5.1.2(i), our privacy policy and App Privacy answers):** loading a YouTube player sends device and network data to Google. That is **third-party data sharing we must disclose and ask permission for** before the first video loads (the privacy-enhanced `youtube-nocookie` embed reduces but does not remove this). The current statement "no tracking or third-party sharing" would stop being true. Plan: a one-line notice and a tap-to-play consent in the existing preview, plus policy and App Privacy changes.
6. **Apple:** Guideline 5.2.2 requires authorization from the service whose content we display (the YouTube terms are that authorization, and Apple can ask us to show it), and 5.2.3 bans saving or downloading third-party media. An app whose headline content is other people's videos also invites a "minimum functionality / repackaged content" question under 4.2; our timer, logging and the rest of the app mitigate that but do not remove it.
7. **No native video features:** no Picture-in-Picture control from us, no HLS caption control, no offline.

## 4. Free, commercially licensed video we host ourselves

- **Public-domain US government video** is the cleanest hosting basis, but what exists is military aerobics in the wrong styles, dated, and may carry third-party music and identifiable people. Not a fit for DanceHall's brand or styles.
- **CC BY video** is the only other no-permission-needed basis. I found **none** in the target styles. If one is found, it needs: the licence version confirmed on the video page, evidence the uploader owns the footage and the music, a release for anyone shown, attribution displayed as the licence requires, and no edits if it is ND. A CC BY-NC or ND-incompatible video is unusable.
- **Written permission from a creator** is the practical way to host or embed anything else. It costs nothing but time, may be refused, and must cover: commercial use in a mobile app, the platform you will use (embed or self-hosting), duration, attribution, takedown, and the music inside the video.
- Self-hosting also needs hosting. Some providers have free tiers that might cover a pilot; none was activated and their terms were not verified.

## 6. Timer, pause/resume and completion without misleading anyone

Today the wall clock drives the session and the renderer follows. With an embedded player we cannot guarantee the video plays smoothly (buffering, an ad, a manual pause inside the player), so for an embedded class **the video must be the master and the clock must only credit time the video is actually playing**:

1. The existing runner stays the single source of truth for blocks, logging and completion. A thin **video-clock adapter** feeds it ticks only while the player reports PLAYING **and its position advances**; each tick is credited at most `min(wall-clock elapsed, video-position elapsed)`, so a stalled, ad-interrupted or skipped video can never invent exercise time.
2. Pause and resume from our controls call the player's pause/play; a pause made inside the player pauses the session, and vice versa.
3. Skip and previous seek the video to the block's start timestamp and move the runner to that block (the block list must be authored from the video's own timestamps).
4. Buffering, an ad, an error or the app leaving the foreground pause the session automatically (the existing backgrounding rule already does the last one).
5. A class **cannot be marked complete early**: completion is the final block reached with credited time, as now. If the player fails, the existing guided-text fallback and timer take over and the saved time is the time actually credited.
6. For a **self-hosted** class the existing clock-master behaviour is kept unchanged.

All of this is design only; nothing is built.

## 7. Provider-independent design (not built)

A class record carries a **source** discriminated by type: `embed_youtube` (video id, start offsets, attribution), `hosted_hls` (manifest, captions), or `none` (guided text). The player chooses the matching renderer, the same session screen and logging serve both, and a class whose source is unavailable, unlicensed or unreviewed falls back to guided text. A class cannot be published without its **proof file** (section 8). Adding a provider later is one renderer.

## 8. Verification worksheet (one per video; nothing is used until complete)

1. Page URL, creator or channel, upload date, length.
2. The licence line on the video page ("Standard YouTube License" or "Creative Commons Attribution"), screenshot saved.
3. Embedding enabled (the video plays on a test page via the official embed).
4. The channel's and website's own terms regarding reuse and commercial use.
5. Music: named track or "original", and where its licence comes from. Description or on-screen credits.
6. Instructor and any performers: named, with evidence they agreed to commercial distribution (or a statement from the owner).
7. Any sponsor, brand or trademark visible (for example the sponsor on charity-campaign videos).
8. For hosting: written permission from the owner covering commercial in-app use, hosting, duration, attribution, takedown.
9. Fitness review of the content by a qualified person who is not the author, noting impact level, beginner suitability and any unsafe moves.
10. Attribution text and the link back to the original, as the terms require.
11. Date checked and by whom.

**Permission-request note (draft, not sent):** introduce DanceHall Fit AI (an adult fitness and nutrition app), name the exact video, say we would like to **embed or host it unedited with full credit and a link back**, state it is free to the creator and to us, ask for written confirmation covering commercial use in a mobile app, the music in the video, removal on request, and who to credit, and say what we will not do (no edits, no re-labelling, no ads over it).

## 9. Recommendation: the safest zero-licensing-cost approach for V1

There is **no cleared free video today**, so the safe approach is staged and keeps the app honest:

1. **Do not publish any video class until its worksheet is complete.** Until then the experience remains guided text with a timer and the listing says so.
2. **Prefer written permission from the creator, in every case.** It is the only thing that covers music, likeness and commercial use, and it works for embedding and for hosting. Start with the leads in section 2 (the charity's Afrobeats beginner classes and Afroletics), and **search for Dancehall and Amapiano creators with normal web access**, because I could not search YouTube directly.
3. **Embed with the official player; do not host, download, edit or re-brand.** That is the only approach that does not depend on a licence we have not verified, and it keeps rights with the creator. Host a video ourselves only if it is public domain or CC BY and fully verified, or the creator grants hosting in writing.
4. **Build provider-independent** (section 7) so the same classes can later move to hosted video if you obtain rights or budget.
5. **Disclose and consent:** YouTube embeds involve Google, so the privacy policy, App Privacy answers and a tap-to-play notice must say so; this changes what the app can claim about third-party data sharing.
6. **Be honest in the listing:** "free classes from named instructors on YouTube", with credit and links, only for classes that are actually there. No claim of exclusive or original classes.

**What this approach cannot give you:** classes authored to your exact needs (beginner pacing, a low-impact version, a specific Dancehall/Amapiano set), guaranteed availability, ad-free playback, or captions you control. If the free route cannot supply Dancehall and Amapiano, V1 may launch with fewer styles. Say so rather than substitute.
