# Review pack: Dancehall and reggae content (DRAFT, not for publication)

**Reviewers:** an exercise professional (safety and progression), Dancehall and reggae practitioners (authenticity and respect), and counsel (rights, likeness, music). The reviewer must not be the author.
**Status:** preparation only. The catalogue contains **no** Dancehall or reggae workouts, and the database currently **cannot store** those styles (proposal M3 in `docs/planning/SQL_PROPOSALS_NOT_APPLIED.md`, not applied). Nothing here is choreography, and no choreography is to be published until the gates in `docs/planning/DANCEHALL_REGGAE_CONTENT_PLAN.md` are met.

## What the engine does today

A person can state Dancehall or reggae as a preferred style. The engine keeps it as a preference, tells them plainly that sessions "are not in the catalogue yet", and shows other styles meanwhile. This keeps the brand visible without pretending content exists.

## Questions for practitioners (none is assumed)

1. Which movements and rhythms define each style, and what vocabulary do practitioners want used, with whose names and credit?
2. What is a respectful way to teach and describe the history and culture briefly and accurately?
3. Which named or viral routines and moves have owners or communities who expect permission or credit?
4. What are the typical tempo ranges the choreography is built for? (The plan should show a tempo range only after the practitioners confirm it.)
5. Which parts are not appropriate to simplify or commercialise, and which language or imagery should be avoided?
6. Who is willing to be credited as an author or instructor, on what terms?

## Questions for the exercise professional

1. Which movements involve deep knee or hip flexion, rapid hip rotation, repeated bounce, or floor-adjacent positions, and what low-impact and seated alternatives are appropriate?
2. What warm-up, cool-down, pacing and rest should each session include, and what intensity labels are honest for each block?
3. What beginner progression across four weeks is reasonable for the intended audience?
4. What stop-and-seek-advice wording is appropriate, without diagnosing?

## Questions for counsel

1. Which agreements are needed for original choreography (assignment or licence, credit, likeness consent, term, territory)?
2. If music is supplied: what licences (master, publishing, synchronisation, in-app and subscription use, territories) and from whom? If music is not supplied: how should tempo guidance be presented?
3. What are the obligations for each country in which the app is activated?

## Proposed minimum launch set (from the content plan, for discussion)

Two Dancehall workouts (10 and 15 minutes), one low-tempo reggae flow (15 minutes, low impact), one seated Dancehall-inspired flow (10 minutes), and one four-week beginner program mixing them with existing styles. Guided text and counts first; music only when the licence route is settled.

## Gates (in order)

Owner decision on authors and budget; written agreements; drafts entered as drafts; independent fitness and safety review and a cultural review; licence and media records; the style migration applied only when the first reviewed workout is ready; publish behind a flag.

## Checklist

Authors credited with consent; likeness and rights agreements signed; low-impact and seated variants for every block; safety notes present; independent reviews recorded; cultural review recorded; language and copy checks; accessibility (text equivalents, reduced motion); flag off by default; owner approval before activation.
